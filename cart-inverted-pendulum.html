Overview

A classic control problem, tested past the simulation stage

The cart-inverted pendulum is a standard proving ground for control theory: keep a rod balanced upright on a cart that can only move left and right, using nothing but the cart's motion to correct it. This study modeled the system from first principles, then compared two classic state-feedback approaches, pole placement and LQR, first in MATLAB simulation and then on real hardware.

5pole/LQR tunings compared in simulation

Results

Faster poles meant faster, but shakier, correction

In simulation, every pole placement and LQR configuration tested was stable, confirming the underlying dynamic model was correct. Poles placed further from the imaginary axis produced faster cart correction at the cost of larger oscillations; poles closer in gave a slower but gentler response. On the real cart-inverted pendulum hardware, though, the pendulum could not hold upright for long, likely due to imperfect sensor readings or initial-condition errors rather than a flaw in the control design itself.
